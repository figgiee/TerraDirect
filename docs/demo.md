## Run the demo

1. Select **Start stream** and wait for **Live**. Select **Present** to open the video-only audience window. Share that window and steer from the original tab. Double-click the video to enter fullscreen. If the browser blocks the window, **Present** enters video-only fullscreen in the original tab and Escape restores the controls.
2. Once the glacier and river bend are visible, select **Meteor impact**. Judges can also type their own direction and select **Apply**.
   **Random event** sends one of 18 landscape directions to the active Orbis stream. The receipt names the selected event.
3. Use **Mark change visible** once the requested change appears at its original landmark. For the river test, use **Open spillway** once **Block creek** has produced a visible diversion.
4. Select **End stream** from the operator tab. Select **Download run** to save the observation time and available before-and-after frames.

The presentation opens in Dynamic at 1080p. If the stream reports capacity, retry from the operator tab. [Model behavior and rehearsal evidence](orbis.md)

Stable made existing field boundaries clearer in the millennia rehearsal. The village stayed small, open fields remained after the forest cue, and the original river bend moved out of view. In a later Dynamic test, one red roof came into view away from the village. A separate Dynamic run demonstrated moonlight on the gorge starting frame. Validate every direction on Satellite before scoring it here.

## Describe a visible change

Use one landmark and one observable change. At the river bend, new field boundaries, groups of rooftops, and broad tree cover are easier to identify than the age of individual buildings.

The year labels request a view that many years after the starting frame. **Slow**, **Fast**, and **Very fast** also send prompt directions. They set no simulation clock. **Tilt-shift** asks for a band of sharp focus through the settlement. Use [Terrain orbit](http://127.0.0.1:5173/?view=terrain) for the square tile.
